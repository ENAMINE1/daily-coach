How to study each topic

AI can write the syntax, so spend your time on judgement. For every topic, ask the same five questions before you move on. Your notes become the scripts for your shorts.

The five questions

  • Problem: what pain does this solve, and what did people do before it?
  • Mechanism: how does it work underneath, at one level below the API?
  • Limits: at what scale, load or failure does it break?
  • Trade-off: what do you give up by choosing it, and what are the alternatives?
  • Interview signal: which question would make an interviewer reach for this?

Use AI properly

  • Let AI write the boilerplate, then read every line and explain it out loud.
  • Break it on purpose: kill the DB, send duplicate requests, add 10k rows, then watch what happens.
  • Ask AI to quiz you ("interview me on isolation levels, one question at a time") instead of having it explain.
  • Check AI claims against official docs. They are often subtly wrong about versions.

Build PayFlow, a small payments and wallet backend. It fits your Mastercard context and touches every topic: accounts, transfers, idempotency, a ledger, auth, events, caching, cloud deployment and an analytics pipeline in Databricks. Each month adds one layer, so by week 24 you have one deep repo instead of ten toy ones.

After Effects: from tools to creative choices

Tool tutorials teach you to copy one effect. This track builds the vocabulary first, then trains the real skill you described: imagining a scene and producing several versions of it, so you can judge which one works. At 30 minutes a day, about 13 hours a month, keep each piece tiny and finish it.

The variation drill (every Friday)

Take one storyboard beat, for example "the logo appears". Make three versions that differ in only one variable: direction (left, right, centre), method (scale, mask reveal, morph), timing (snappy or floaty) or camera (static, push in). Put them side by side and write one line on why the winner wins. After 24 Fridays you have 70+ deliberate comparisons, which is how taste gets trained.

Creativity prompts for when you're stuck

  • Constrain it: only 2 colours, only one shape, only scale.
  • Steal a motion from nature: a door, a drop of water, a page turning.
  • Change the verb: instead of "appears", try "assembles", "unfolds" or "is pushed in".
  • Reverse it: animate the exit first, then design the entrance to match.

Turning the plan into shorts

You're right that short-form tech content is rarely structured. Publish this roadmap as a numbered series, so each video is one step on a visible map. Your Sunday notes already hold the scripts.

Five repeatable formats

  • "What breaks if…" e.g. what breaks if two people withdraw at the same second.
  • "Interviewer says X, means Y", straight from the intent map.
  • "Under the hood in 45s": HashMap, B+ tree, JWT, Kafka partition.
  • "Week N of 26": an honest progress log, which builds a following.
  • "One analogy": a single everyday picture that makes one concept click.

Where the two tracks meet

From month 4 onward, animate your tech shorts in After Effects: a request moving through a load balancer, a cache hit and miss, a saga rolling back. It's the most useful AE practice you can get, because you start from a storyboard, not a tutorial, and it makes your channel look unlike anyone else's.

Production checklist

  • Hook in the first 2 seconds: say the problem, not the topic name.
  • One idea per short, 30–60 seconds long.
  • End on the interview line: "so if they ask X, say Y".
  • Batch-record on Sunday, edit two at a time.
